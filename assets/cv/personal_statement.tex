  {\LARGE\bfseries Personal Statement}\\[0.3em]
  {\large Majid Ghasemi, University of Waterloo}
\end{center}

I am a second-year PhD student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Waterloo, advised by Mark Crowley. My thesis asks whether reinforcement learning agents can become ethical by default: not because deviation is penalized, but because virtuous behavior has been acquired as a stable disposition through social learning among agents that already behave well. Getting there requires three kinds of work that rarely sit in the same room: formal analysis of when a social feedback signal can be trusted, multi-agent environments where ethical and epistemic failures are coupled, and an honest account of what ``ethical disposition'' means when borrowed from virtue ethics into a policy. I am applying to the AAAI Doctoral Consortium because I am at the point where those three strands have to become one thesis, and I want that to be scrutinized by people outside my lab before I commit to it.

\textbf{What I expect to gain.} I will defend my thesis proposal by the end of March 2027, about five weeks after the consortium. The most useful thing the DC can give me is pressure on the proposal itself. My completed work is mostly diagnostic and theoretical: a formal account of feedback collapse, regret bounds for learning whom to trust in a sycophantic social layer, and the Forager's Dilemma environment. The remaining work, virtue by observation, is empirical and riskier. I would value a mentor's view on three specific questions. First, whether disposition retention under partner-swap and incentive-flip interventions is a convincing operationalization of internalization, or whether reviewers will read it as mimicry with extra steps. Second, how far the gridworld results need to scale before the claim is credible to the broader AAAI community. Third, how to position work that draws on both multi-agent RL and moral philosophy so that it is legible to both audiences rather than dismissed by each. I also want frank advice on the academic path. I intend to pursue a postdoc and then a faculty position, and I would like to hear from established researchers how they weighed venue choice, collaboration, and breadth versus depth at my stage.

\textbf{What I can contribute.} I can offer other participants a working bridge between machine ethics and mainstream RL theory. Much of my recent work is in bandits and regret analysis, so I can give concrete technical feedback to students working on alignment, human feedback, or multi-agent coordination, and I can push back constructively when an ethical claim is doing more work than the experiments support. I have presented this research to philosophers (an invited talk at Formal Ethics 2026) and to RL researchers (EWRL 2026 and two ICML 2026 workshops), and I have learned how the same result has to be framed differently for each. I am also genuinely interested in other people's problems, and I expect the sessions where I am not presenting to be the most valuable ones at the AIES 2026 Student Program in October. I will bring that same energy to the two days at AAAI.

\textbf{Prior and other consortia.} I will participate in the AIES 2026 Student Program in Malm\"o in October 2026, where I will present an earlier version of this research plan. I am not applying to other doctoral consortia for 2027.
